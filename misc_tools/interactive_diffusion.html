Viscosity of aqueous glycerol

Model, data & assumptions

Stokes–Einstein: D = kBT / (3πηd), with d the hydrodynamic diameter. Mean-squared displacement in n dimensions: ⟨r²⟩ = 2nDΔt.

Viscosity at 20 °C (fit to Takamura et al. 2012, Table 10, zero salinity), with x the glycerol mass fraction:
η20(x) = ηw · exp[(Ax + Bx²) / (1 + Cx)], ηw = 1.002 mPa·s, A = 2.5139, B = 0.0712, C = −0.6208.

Temperature: the Takamura data are at 20 °C only. For other temperatures the 20 °C fit is scaled by the ratio η(T, x)/η(20 °C, x) from the Cheng (2008) correlation (Ind. Eng. Chem. Res. 47, 3285). At 20 °C the factor is exactly 1.

Range: data span 0–91 % w/w. Above that the fit is an extrapolation and underestimates viscosity (it gives ~916 mPa·s for pure glycerol; the measured value is ~1410 mPa·s at 20 °C).
